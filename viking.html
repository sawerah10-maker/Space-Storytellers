MISSION 04 · 1976

Viking 1 Lander

The pioneer that explored Mars and remained on the Red Planet.

THE MISSION BEGINS

A New Chapter in Mars Exploration

NASA's Viking 1 spacecraft landed on Mars on July 20, 1976. It became the first successful U.S. mission to land and operate on the Martian surface.

The lander carried cameras, scientific instruments and a robotic arm to collect and study samples of Martian soil.

Searching for Signs of Life

Viking 1 performed experiments to investigate whether life could exist in Martian soil. Although the experiments produced interesting results, they did not provide conclusive evidence of life on Mars.

What Was Left Behind?

Viking 1 continued sending information to Earth for more than six years. Communication with the lander ended in November 1982. The spacecraft remains on the surface of Mars, preserving its place in space exploration history.
