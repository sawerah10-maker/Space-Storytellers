THE STORY BEHIND THE EQUIPMENT

Exploring the Moon on Wheels

The Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV) was an electric-powered vehicle developed for NASA's Apollo missions. It allowed astronauts to travel farther across the lunar surface and explore areas that were difficult to reach on foot.

When Was It First Used?

The first Lunar Roving Vehicle was used during Apollo 15 in 1971. It was later used in Apollo 16 and Apollo 17. Each rover helped astronauts collect lunar rocks, take photographs and conduct scientific investigations.

Why Was It Left on the Moon?

The Lunar Roving Vehicles were not designed to return to Earth. After each mission, astronauts left the vehicles behind on the lunar surface before returning to their spacecraft.

Three Rovers, Three Missions

NASA sent three Lunar Roving Vehicles to the Moon through Apollo 15, Apollo 16 and Apollo 17. All three remain on the lunar surface at their respective landing sites.

A Lasting Legacy

These electric rovers helped astronauts explore greater distances and collect important scientific samples. Today, they remain reminders of the Apollo missions and human exploration of the Moon.
